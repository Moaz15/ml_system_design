USER LIFECYCLE
==============

WHAT BELONGS HERE
-----------------
Churn prediction, lifetime value, subscription conversion and renewal,
reactivation, propensity and uplift models, segmentation, pricing and
discount targeting.

THE SKILL THIS FOLDER DRILLS
----------------------------
The label is defined by a WINDOW you choose, and the prediction only
matters if it drives an intervention that changes the outcome. Both facts
are routinely skipped by candidates, and both decide the whole design.

  - defining churn: 30 days inactive? failed renewal? a self-serve cancel?
    Each definition builds a different model and a different product
  - the prediction is worthless without the action attached to it - who
    gets the discount, the call, the reactivation push
  - uplift over propensity: target the persuadable, not the likely
  - survival analysis and censoring - users who have not churned YET
  - the self-defeating prophecy: a good model plus a good intervention
    destroys its own accuracy, so accuracy is the wrong success metric
  - long label windows versus the need to act early
  - LTV as a heavy-tailed regression where the mean is a bad summary

CANDIDATE CASES
---------------
  01  SUBSCRIPTION RENEWAL AND THE DISCOUNT THAT PAYS          HARD
      WRITTEN -> churn_prediction/  (five files, start at _index.txt)
      Subscription churn prediction and the retention offer it drives.
      Decide, for each subscriber reaching the end of a billing cycle,
      whether to intervene and at what discount depth, under a fixed
      margin budget.
      Drills the whole folder skill at once: a label defined by a
      window you chose (renewed within [T, T+3]), a prediction that is
      worthless without the action attached to it, uplift over
      propensity, and the self-defeating prophecy that makes accuracy
      the wrong success metric.
      The trap: ranking by P(lapse) and spending the budget on Lost
      Causes, Sure Things and Sleeping Dogs, while reporting a strong
      AUC and a strong renewal rate among treated users.
      Second trap, for whoever avoids the first: fitting the uplift
      model on observational history in which every discount was
      assigned by a risk rule, so depth and risk are the same variable.

  02  Free-to-paid conversion propensity with a 14-day trial.
  03  Discount targeting: uplift modelling under a fixed budget.
      Note the overlap with 01 - keep the pure budget-constrained
      uplift drill here, and let 01 carry propensity-plus-action, so
      the "propensity is the wrong target" reveal lands once.

  Case files for a single case may live in a sub-folder when the case
  is carried across several files, as 01 is. Flat
  <domain>_case_NN.txt files remain the default.
