CASE 01 - PROBLEM: Subscription Renewal and the Discount That Pays
==================================================================
CONTEXT
-------
A subscription audio-content app in India. Long-form spoken content:
audiobooks, serialised fiction, regional-language originals. Revenue is
recurring - every billing cycle ends in a renewal or a lapse.

  - 2.5M renewal decisions a month
  - headline plan Rs 299/month; a long-tenure plan at about Rs 108 per
    cycle; annual plans are a separate population
  - renewal rate 35-50% depending on cohort, plan and tenure. It is not
    one number and should not be modelled as one
  - about 60% of the base is on autopay; the rest renew by an explicit
    action every cycle
  - three years of billing history at cycle grain, app events at
    session grain
  - payment failures and dunning retries account for a real slice of
    non-renewal, and that slice is a different problem


THE TWO LEVERS, AND WHY THEY FIGHT
----------------------------------
At each renewal the business controls exactly two things: RENEWAL
PROBABILITY and EFFECTIVE PRICE. A discount raises the first and lowers
the second.

    maximise renewal rate  ->  discount everybody, margin collapses
    maximise price         ->  discount nobody, the fence-sitters leave

So the objective is EXPECTED REVENUE PER SUBSCRIBER. Renewal rate is
not the objective, and a policy that lowers it while raising revenue
per subscriber is a good policy. Someone in the room will have to be
told that out loud.


WHAT EXISTS TODAY
-----------------
A CRM rule: listening in the last 30 days below a hand-set threshold
gets a blanket 20% off, pushed three days before renewal. The threshold
was set by a PM eighteen months ago and has not moved.

No holdout. Everyone who trips the rule gets the offer, so nobody can
say what the rule is worth. The dashboard reports that offer recipients
renew well, which is selection, not effect.

THE FACT THE CASE TURNS ON: every discount in three years came from
that rule, so discount depth is collinear with being at risk. Nowhere
in the history did a randomly chosen subscriber get a randomly chosen
depth.


DATA
----
  - the billing ledger is clean and authoritative: attempted,
    succeeded, failed, mandate revoked, user cancelled
  - app events settle over about 48 hours, which is fine for 30-day
    aggregates and matters for the snapshot timing in 02_label.txt
  - the CRM logs offers SENT, inconsistently, plus redemptions. It does
    not log who was considered and skipped, so even the historical
    treatment assignment is only half recorded

WHY THIS IS ON YOUR DESK NOW
----------------------------
Finance wants the discount budget justified or cut. Growth wants a
churn model, because that is the thing people ask for. These are not
the same request, and the second does not answer the first.

YOUR ROLE
---------
Senior IC, or a lead with one engineer and one analyst. You own the
design, the sequencing, and the argument about what not to build.

QUESTION
--------
"We want to reduce subscription churn. Design a churn prediction system
that tells us who is going to cancel, so we can send them a retention
offer."

Take the question as asked, then say whether it is the right one.


#######################################################################
WHICH PROJECT IS THIS
#######################################################################

The three readings share a dataset and share nothing else.

  READING                OUTPUT                 VERDICT
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  A churn propensity     P(lapse) per user      a score alone drives
                                                no decision
  B price sensitivity    renewal curve against  pricing work, and
                         discount depth         half the answer
  C uplift-targeted      a treat / no-treat     RECOMMENDED - it
    offer                policy with a depth    contains A and B

C, with B's price-response curve inside it and A demoted to an
eligibility filter and a monitoring series. Saying this in the first
three minutes IS the case. Everything after it is execution.

THE DECISION, STATED AS A DECISION
#######################################################################

    For each subscriber approaching the end of a billing cycle, decide
    whether to intervene, with what offer, at what discount depth,
    automatically, at scale, within a fixed margin budget.

Success is not AUC. Success is INCREMENTAL RETAINED REVENUE PER RUPEE
OF DISCOUNT SPENT, measured against a randomised holdout.

Four properties of that decision drive the whole design:

  - it is per (user, cycle), not per user
  - the renewal date is known weeks ahead, so nothing here needs
    real-time inference
  - the budget is fixed, so this is an allocation problem and the
    output is a RANKING, not independent yes/no calls
  - the offer is irreversible: it moves the user's reference price, and
    that cost lands in the NEXT cycle rather than this one


CLARIFYING QUESTIONS  (* = changes the answer entirely)
#######################################################################

  Q1 *  Any historical randomisation at all - an A/B, a holdout, a
        period with the rule switched off? With it, an uplift model
        fits in week three. Without it, phase one is not modelling, it
        is buying identification with deliberately randomised spend,
        and the timeline doubles.

  Q2    What is the discount budget, per month, in rupees? Without it
        there is no allocation problem and no stopping rule.

  Q3 *  May we leave a group untreated permanently? An always-on
        holdout is the only honest measurement of the channel, and it
        costs real retained revenue every month. If the answer is no,
        say plainly that the system can be built but never evaluated,
        and let the budget holder own that trade.

  Q4    Price discount only, or also content, a free extension, a plan
        downgrade? Each extra arm multiplies the sample needed.

  Q5    How much of churn is INVOLUNTARY - dead card, revoked mandate,
        insufficient balance? A discount does not fix a dead card. At
        30% of lapses, the highest-return month of work is dunning and
        mandate repair, not a model.


#######################################################################
THE TWO TRAPS
#######################################################################

1. Rank by P(lapse), send offers down the ranking until the budget is
   gone, report a strong AUC and a strong renewal rate among treated
   users. That policy spends most of the budget on the people least
   likely to move, pays people who would have renewed anyway, and
   reminds passive auto-renewers that they are paying. None of it is
   visible in AUC, because AUC scores an outcome and the business is
   buying a CHANGE in that outcome.

2. For whoever avoids the first: fit the uplift model on the
   observational history. Depth and risk are the same variable there,
   so the model learns - confidently - that discounts cause churn, and
   ends up measuring the rule rather than the offer.
