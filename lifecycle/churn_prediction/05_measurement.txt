CASE 01 - MEASUREMENT, NUMBERS AND THE 60-SECOND VERSION
========================================================
The part the project is actually judged on. A churn model is judged on
AUC; this system cannot be, and what replaces it has to be stated
before anything launches.


#######################################################################
1. THE METRIC STACK
#######################################################################

  METRIC                      WHAT IT ANSWERS
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  Qini / AUUC                 does ranking by tau beat random
                              targeting, at every budget level
  Incremental revenue per     THE PRIMARY BUSINESS METRIC, and the
  rupee of discount spent     only number the budget holder is shown
  Calibration of mu_0         is the baseline probability
                              trustworthy - it sets the price term,
                              so a mis-calibrated mu_0 mis-ranks the
                              entire allocation
  Guardrails                  ARPU, gross margin, unsubscribe rate,
                              complaint rate, notification opt-out
                              rate

Primary offline metric: Qini. Primary online metric: incremental
revenue per rupee of discount. Not renewal rate, at any point, in
either place.


#######################################################################
2. QINI, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
#######################################################################

Sort the holdout by predicted tau. Walk down that list, and at each
point plot cumulative INCREMENTAL renewals - treated renewals minus
control renewals, scaled for the group size difference - against the
share of the population targeted.

A diagonal line means the model is worthless: targeting the top decile
by tau is no better than targeting a random decile. A steep early rise
means the Persuadables really are at the top of the ranking, which is
the only claim this project makes. A curve that rises and then FALLS
means the tail of the ranking contains negative uplift - Sleeping Dogs
- and the falling point tells you exactly where the policy should stop
treating, independent of budget.

AUUC is the area under that curve, used for comparing model versions.
Report the curve, not the scalar, because the shape carries the
decision and the scalar does not.

The right way to read it: the Qini curve IS the budget allocation
question, drawn. Any budget corresponds to a point on the x-axis, and
the curve says what that budget buys.


#######################################################################
3. THE ALWAYS-ON HOLDOUT
#######################################################################

A permanently untreated slice, randomised at (user, cycle) assignment,
with no contact of any kind through this channel.

It costs real money every month - those are renewals not saved - and
it is the only thing in the system that can answer "is the whole
channel worth anything". Without it, every reported number is the
treated group's renewal rate, which is selection, not effect.

Three design points worth stating:

  - randomise at assignment (T-7), and analyse on ASSIGNMENT, not on
    delivery. Conditioning on whether the push landed conditions on a
    post-treatment variable correlated with engagement
  - hold the slice out at the USER level across cycles, not
    independently per cycle, or the long-run anchoring and fatigue
    effects leak between arms and the holdout stops being clean
  - size it from the effect you need to detect, and say the number: a
    few percentage points on a base around 0.4 needs tens of
    thousands of users per arm, which at 2.5M monthly decisions is a
    small single-digit percentage

The question to ask the business in week one, from 01_problem.txt Q3:
are we allowed to leave a group untreated permanently? If the answer
is no, the system can still be built, but it can never be evaluated,
and that trade-off belongs to whoever owns the budget rather than to
the person building the model.


#######################################################################
4. THE COMPARISON THAT SETTLES THE ARGUMENT
#######################################################################

Three policies, scored on the same holdout, at the same time:

  POLICY                    DISCOUNT    RENEWAL   REVENUE PER
                            SPEND       RATE      SUBSCRIBER
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  No offer at all           zero        baseline  baseline
  Blanket 20% to everyone   high        HIGHEST   LOWER - margin
  flagged at risk                                 burned on Sure
  (the incumbent rule)                            Things
  Uplift-targeted           LOW         middle    HIGHEST

The headline claim is the third row beating the second AT LOWER SPEND.

The row that has to be defended in the room is the renewal rate column:
the targeted policy renews FEWER people than blanket discounting. That
is not a regression. It is the margin being kept - the renewals given
up are the ones that were being bought back at a price higher than they
were worth. Expect this to be the hardest sentence in the review, and
have the per-subscriber revenue number ready before saying it.


#######################################################################
5. BREAK-EVEN: WHY NOT JUST DISCOUNT HARDER
#######################################################################

The minimum uplift that makes a given depth pay for itself:

    required tau  =  p0 * d / (1 - 2d)

where p0 is the baseline renewal probability and d the depth.

  BASELINE RENEWAL    10% OFF     20% OFF     30% OFF
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  0.35                4.4 pp      11.7 pp     26.2 pp
  0.50                6.2 pp      16.7 pp     37.5 pp

Read the bottom-right cell. At a 50% baseline, a 30% discount has to
move renewal by 37.5 percentage points before it breaks even. Effects
that large do not exist in this population.

This is the cleanest available answer to "why not just discount
harder", and it explains two things that otherwise look like modelling
artefacts: why the optimiser almost never selects the deepest depth,
and why the realised depth mix lands around 27% shallow and 73% mid.


#######################################################################
6. DEFENSIBLE NUMBERS
#######################################################################

Simulated on inputs shaped like this business: roughly 2.5M monthly
renewal events, a 10/20/30 depth grid, a saturating dose-response, and
a sweep over the four quantities that cannot be read off revenue data -
price, baseline renewal rate, maximum uplift, and Persuadable share.

SENSITIVITY SWEEP

  TREATED SHARE   INCREMENTAL REVENUE     SHARE OF INCREMENTAL
                  PER DISCOUNT RUPEE      RENEWALS CAPTURED
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  top 5%          0.83 - 1.64             45 - 81%
  top 10%         0.68 - 1.38             80 - 100%

  - users with ANY profitable depth: 7.4% to 14.3%. Past that the
    policy simply stops - there is nobody left worth treating at any
    depth, at any budget
  - blanket 20% to everyone: MINUS 0.66 per rupee. Value-destructive,
    and it is the comparison that makes the targeted policy worth
    describing at all

RECOMMENDED FIGURES TO QUOTE

  SYMBOL  VALUE                       WHY IT IS DEFENSIBLE
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  A       1.4 incremental retained    upper-middle of the 0.83-1.64
          revenue per discount rupee  band at the top 5%
  X       60% of incremental          conservative against the
          renewals                    45-81% band
  Y       the top 5% of subscribers   matches where the ROI peaks

Do not claim above roughly 2.0 per rupee. At these price points and
baseline renewal rates the arithmetic does not support it, and the
break-even table in section 5 is how someone will show that it does
not.

TWO FACTS THAT WILL BE TESTED

ROI IS INDEPENDENT OF PRICE. The sweep returns identical ratios at Rs
99, Rs 108 and Rs 299, because price appears in both the numerator and
the denominator of gain/cost and cancels. A higher price point cannot
be used to justify a higher ROI claim, and offering one is a quick way
to lose the room.

THE POLICY TREATS WHO IT CLAIMS TO. Inside the treated top 20% of the
simulation: 96% Persuadables, 4% neutral, 0% Sure Things, 0% Sleeping
Dogs. The suppression is not a rule bolted on afterwards - it falls out
of ranking by gain/cost.

HONESTY CONSTRAINT

These are SIMULATION OUTPUTS UNDER STATED ASSUMPTIONS, not measured
results. Quoted anywhere outside this folder they are either labelled
"projected" and framed as design targets, or they are replaced with
real holdout numbers once the policy has run. Presenting a simulated
ROI as a measured one fails at the first "how did you measure that?",
and that is the first question a good interviewer asks.


#######################################################################
7. THE 60-SECOND VERSION
#######################################################################

They asked for a churn model. A churn score drives no decision, so the
deliverable is a treat / do-not-treat policy with a discount depth,
under a margin budget. The question is not who will churn, it is whose
renewal decision an offer changes.

One row is one (user, cycle), features frozen at T-7, label is renewed
within three days of the renewal date so that dunning retries are not
mislabelled as churn. The column that matters is the randomly assigned
arm, and we do not have it - every discount in three years was given by
a risk rule, so depth is collinear with risk and nothing causal is
identified. So phase zero is randomised spend on a budgeted slice: pure
cost, buying identification. The randomisation is the asset, not the
model.

Then three models. A calibrated GBM for the baseline renewal
probability, used for eligibility and for the price term, not for
targeting. A T- or X-learner for uplift, fit only on randomised rows. A
monotonic dose-response across a 10/20/30 grid. The policy maximises
expected revenue, ranks by gain over cost, and treats down the list
until the budget is gone - which means most users get nothing, and the
highest-risk users are often skipped.

It is judged on Qini offline and on incremental revenue per discount
rupee against a permanent holdout, never on AUC, because a working
intervention destroys the accuracy of the model that drove it. I would
expect to treat roughly the top 5% at about 1.4 rupees retained per
rupee spent, against minus 0.66 for the blanket 20% rule running today
- and I would expect the targeted policy to show a LOWER renewal rate
than that rule, which is the margin being kept rather than a
regression.

Week one is not a model. It is the cycle table, the point-in-time
feature layer, and splitting involuntary churn out to payments, because
a discount does not fix a dead card.
