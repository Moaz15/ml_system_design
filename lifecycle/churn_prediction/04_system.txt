CASE 01 - SYSTEM DESIGN, BOOTSTRAP AND FAILURE MODES
====================================================
Drawn only after 02_label.txt and 03_modelling.txt are settled. The
shape is boring on purpose: the renewal date is known weeks in advance,
so there is no real-time requirement anywhere in this system and
nothing is gained by engineering for one.


#######################################################################
1. ARCHITECTURE
#######################################################################

                           OFFLINE (daily batch)

  L0 SOURCES
    billing ledger | app events | payment mandates | offer and
    treatment log | catalog and content metadata
                              |
                              v
  L1 INGEST AND STORE
    ELT into the warehouse, daily partitions, cycle-level grain.
    Unit of analysis = (user_id, cycle_id). Snapshot at T-7, label
    observed at T+3.
                              |
                              v
  L2 FEATURE LAYER
    ONE transform codebase feeding both the offline point-in-time
    table and the online store.
    tenure | consumption | payment health | price-paid history |
    past-offer exposure
                              |
          +-------------------+-------------------+
          v                   v                   v
    M1 PROPENSITY       M2 UPLIFT / CATE     M3 PRICE-RESPONSE
    P(renew | x)        tau(x, arm)          tau(x, d) dose-
    calibrated GBM      T-/X-learner or      response across
    eligibility and     causal forest, on    depths, monotonicity
    monitoring only     RANDOMISED rows      constrained
          |                   |                   |
          +-------------------+-------------------+
                              v
  L4 POLICY / OPTIMISER
    value(x,d) = tau(x,d) * price * (1-d), net of cannibalisation
                 on Sure Things
    argmax over d, subject to SUM(discount cost) <= margin budget
                 (Lagrangian, or greedy knapsack on gain/cost)
    plus suppression: sleeping-dog guard, contact caps, fraud,
                 recent-offer cooldown, active dunning
    plus RANDOMISATION: global holdout percentage and an epsilon
                 exploration arm
                              |
                              v
                            ONLINE
  L5 DECISION SERVICE
    batch scores written to the online store. /decide returns
    {treat?, arm, depth, expiry}. Idempotent per (user, cycle).
    Every call logged, including the no-treat calls.
                              |
                              v
  L6 DELIVERY
    push | in-app paywall | SMS | retention price on the renewal
    screen
                              |
                              v
  L7 MEASUREMENT
    always-on holdout -> Qini and AUUC, incremental revenue per
    rupee of discount, guardrails on ARPU, margin, unsubscribe rate
    and complaint rate
                              |
                              +--> back into L1: assignment and
                                   outcome logs become the next
                                   training set

The loop at the bottom is the system. Exploration produces the data
that the next model is identified from, which is why the epsilon arm
and the holdout are permanent infrastructure rather than a launch
phase.

LATENCY AND RUNTIME BUDGET. The daily batch runs at 06:00 on data
settled to 48 hours, and the whole pipeline has about four hours before
the morning send window - roughly 45 minutes of scoring and 15 minutes
of optimisation at 2.5M rows is comfortable. The /decide call is a
lookup against a precomputed decision, so a p99 of 50ms is generous.
Writing those numbers down matters less here than in a ranking system;
saying WHY they do not matter is the point.

this project has been seen to fail, and the component exists to make
that failure visible rather than to make it impossible.


#######################################################################
3. THE CHICKEN AND EGG, AND HOW TO PAY FOR IT
#######################################################################

M2 and M3 need randomised treat/control data. On day one, none exists -
every historical discount came from the risk rule, so depth and risk
are the same variable and nothing causal is identified from three years
of history.

PHASE 0 - EXPLORE (weeks 1 to 8)
  M1 for eligibility only. On a budgeted slice of the population,
  assign depth at random from the grid, including a true zero-offer
  control. This phase is PURE COST. It buys identification and nothing
  else, and saying that plainly - rather than dressing it up as a
  model-building phase - is what gets it approved honestly.
  Ship alongside it: the dunning and mandate-repair work from
  02_label.txt section 7, which needs no model and pays for part of
  the exploration budget.

PHASE 1 - LEARN (weeks 9 to 14)
  Fit M2 and M3 on the Phase-0 logs. Validate by Qini on a held-out
  slice of those logs, never on rows the model was fit on. Expect the
  first Qini curve to be disappointing and say so in advance; a flat
  curve at this stage means the sample is thin, not that uplift does
  not exist.

PHASE 2 - EXPLOIT (week 15 onward)
  The optimiser targets Persuadables under the budget. Retain the
  global holdout and the epsilon arm FOREVER, so the next model stays
  identified and the channel stays measurable.

NON-NEGOTIABLE: the randomisation is the data asset, not the model. The
model can be refit in an afternoon. A quarter of randomised assignment
logs cannot be recovered after the fact at any price.

WHAT GETS SHIPPED WHEN, COMPRESSED

  WEEK 1-2    cycle table, point-in-time feature layer, logging. No
              model. Separate involuntary from voluntary churn and
              hand the involuntary slice to payments
  WEEK 3-4    M1 calibrated, running in shadow. Randomiser live on a
              small slice
  WEEK 5-8    exploration accumulates. The only thing reported is
              assignment balance and delivery rate
  WEEK 9-14   M2 and M3 fit; Qini on held-out logs; the three-policy
              comparison from 05_measurement.txt section 4 run
              offline against logged assignments
  WEEK 15     policy live at a small share against the incumbent rule
              plus a holdout, ramped on the incremental-revenue
              metric and nothing else
  MONTH 6     depth grid revisited, arm types beyond price
              considered, per-segment Qini monitored

DELIBERATELY NOT IN SCOPE: real-time scoring, coin unlocks and episode
paywalls, a continuous depth optimiser, and any per-user LTV model. The
last one is worth naming out loud because it is the thing people ask
for next, and it does not change a single decision this system makes.


#######################################################################
4. FAILURE MODES AND MONITORING
#######################################################################

THE SELF-DEFEATING PROPHECY. A good model plus a good intervention
destroys its own accuracy: the people predicted to lapse are treated,
they renew, and the churn model now looks wrong. If anyone is tracking
M1's AUC as the success metric, a working system reads as a degrading
one. This is the single strongest reason the success metric lives in
L7 and is measured against a holdout. Monitor M1 on the HOLDOUT, where
the prophecy is not self-defeating because nothing was done.

DISCOUNT ANCHORING. Treating someone this cycle changes their reference
price for the next one. The effect is a cost that lands outside the
cycle being optimised, so a myopic per-cycle optimiser will
systematically over-treat. Watch renewal rate at FULL price among users
treated in prior cycles, against the holdout, and if the gap grows the
grid is too deep or the cooldown is too short.

OFFER FATIGUE. More contact, less response, and eventually a disabled
notification channel or an uninstall - a permanent loss driven by a
short-term metric. There is no direct fatigue label, so it is enforced
as a constraint (contact caps, cooldown windows) and monitored through
channel-level opt-out rate rather than modelled.

CONFOUNDED REFIT. The most likely way this system dies quietly: six
months in, someone refits M2 on all available data rather than on
randomised rows only, because there is so much more of it. Depth is
collinear with risk in those rows, the model learns that discounts
cause churn, and the policy inverts. The defence is mechanical, not
cultural: M2's training job reads from a view that contains only
randomised assignments.

DRIFT. Monitor input distributions, not just outcomes, because labels
arrive three to ten days late and a drifted population is visible
immediately. Watch the share of autopay users, the tenure mix, and the
distribution of minutes_30d.

ASSIGNMENT IMBALANCE. Check the randomiser daily against its intended
split. A silently broken randomiser produces a dataset that looks fine
and is worth nothing, and it is usually found months later.

FALLBACK WHEN THE MODEL IS DOWN. Serve no offer. Not the old rule - no
offer. A missed save offer costs one cycle of one user's margin; a
stale or duplicated offer costs money, trains a user to wait for
discounts, and corrupts the assignment log. Silence is the safe
default here, and it is a defensible answer precisely because it is the
cheap direction of error.
