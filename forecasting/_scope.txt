FORECASTING
===========

WHAT BELONGS HERE
-----------------
Demand, traffic, revenue, capacity, inventory, staffing, infrastructure
load. Anything indexed by time where the question is "how much, when".

THE SKILL THIS FOLDER DRILLS
----------------------------
Time is the axis, so every shortcut that works elsewhere breaks here. A
random train/test split leaks the future. The cost of error is almost
never symmetric, so squared error is usually the wrong loss.

  - horizon and granularity drive everything: hourly next-day and monthly
    next-year are different products
  - backtesting with rolling origin; no random splits, ever
  - seasonality, holidays, promotions and one-off events as features
  - hierarchical forecasts that must reconcile: SKU to category to total
  - quantile loss and prediction intervals, because the decision needs a
    range and an asymmetric cost, not a point estimate
  - intermittent and spiky series in the long tail, where the mean is
    almost never the right thing to predict
  - censored history: when past capacity limited what was served, the
    series records what you COULD do, not what was wanted
  - cold start for new items with no history
  - classical baselines (naive, seasonal naive, ETS) that ML must beat,
    and frequently does not

CANDIDATE CASES
---------------
Five cases, ordered by difficulty. Each drills a different forecasting
failure mode. Industry is tagged because the same spine has to survive
content, media and supply chain without changing shape.

  01  LISTENING HOURS FOR CAPACITY PLANNING            [CONTENT]  EASY
      WRITTEN -> forecasting_case_01.txt
      Forecast daily listening hours and peak concurrent streams 90 days
      out, to size CDN and transcode capacity and to set the content
      release calendar.
      Drills the spine end to end on a clean, dense, strongly seasonal
      series: choosing horizon and granularity, weekly and festival
      seasonality, rolling-origin backtesting, and being forced to beat
      seasonal naive before any ML is allowed in the room.
      The trap: quoting MAPE on a series with a strong weekly cycle and
      calling the model good when seasonal naive is within a point of it.

  02  AD INVENTORY FORECASTING FOR SALES COMMITMENTS   [MEDIA]  MEDIUM
      Sales must promise advertisers a guaranteed number of impressions
      per targeting segment, one to three months ahead. Over-promise and
      you owe makegoods; under-promise and you leave revenue unsold.
      Drills hierarchical forecasting over OVERLAPPING, non-nested
      segments (age x city x genre) that still have to reconcile to the
      total, and QUANTILE forecasting as the actual product - sales should
      be selling the P20, not the mean. The output feeds a booking system,
      not a dashboard, so the interface is part of the design.

  03  DEMAND FOR A TITLE THAT DOES NOT EXIST YET       [CONTENT]  MEDIUM
      The commissioning team wants first-90-day listening hours for a
      proposed show, before it is made, to decide whether to fund it.
      Drills forecasting with NO SERIES. There is no history for this
      title, so this is cross-sectional regression over metadata and
      analogues, pooled across the catalog - not a time series model at
      all. Recognising that the time-series framing is wrong IS the case.
      Second layer: the forecast changes the outcome it predicts, because
      a high forecast buys the show the promotion that makes it true.

  04  SHIPMENT VOLUME PER LANE FOR COURIER CAPACITY    [SUPPLY CHAIN]  HARD
      A shipping aggregator books pickup and line-haul capacity with
      courier partners 7 to 14 days ahead, per pickup city and courier.
      Under-book and shipments sit in backlog and sellers churn;
      over-book and you pay for empty trucks.
      Drills the full hierarchy (seller -> city -> zone -> national) with
      reconciliation, intermittent and spiky demand across the long tail
      of small sellers, ASYMMETRIC cost where the two error directions are
      priced differently, and the fact that the forecast is an input to an
      allocation optimiser rather than the decision itself.
      Structural breaks are routine, not exceptional: one large seller
      signing or leaving moves a whole city's series overnight, so the
      model needs a stated policy for them.

  05  PEAK SEASON CAPACITY: FORECASTING THE SALE       [SUPPLY CHAIN]  HARD
      Plan headcount, warehouse space and courier contracts for the
      festive sale season, committed two to three months ahead and
      effectively irreversible once signed.
      Drills forecasting an event you have only observed three or four
      times. The peak dominates the year, so ordinary seasonality methods
      have almost no data for the only part anyone cares about, and the
      promo and event calendar becomes the model.
      The insight the case turns on: you observe SHIPMENTS, not DEMAND.
      Last peak was capacity-constrained, so the history is CENSORED - it
      records what you could handle, not what was wanted. Train on it
      naively and you will under-build forever, every year.
