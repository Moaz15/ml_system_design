CASE 01 - CORE: Committing to Capacity You Cannot Return
========================================================
Read this one until it is boring. The full case (forecasting_case_01.txt)
is the same problem with realistic mess added; do not open it until the
five moves below are automatic.


THE PROBLEM
-----------
An audio app streams about 900,000 listening hours a day. Listening is
growing, and it has a strong weekly cycle - weekends well above midweek.

The CDN contract works like this. Once a month, you declare how many
gigabytes you will use next month. You pay Rs 1.60 per GB for everything
you declared, used or not. Anything beyond what you declared is billed at
2.2x, so Rs 3.52 per GB. Unused commitment is not refunded.

Today, finance takes last month's number, multiplies by a growth factor,
adds 15% "for safety", and declares that. Last year they went over in 4
months and left commitment unused in the other 8.

    What number do you declare?


THE FIVE MOVES
--------------
Every one of these transfers to problems that have nothing to do with
forecasting. That is why this case comes first.

  M1  FIND THE DECISION
      A prediction that changes no action is decoration. Name the action,
      its lead time, and how often it repeats. Here: one number, declared
      30 days ahead, locked for a month.

  M2  PRICE BOTH DIRECTIONS OF ERROR
      Declaring too little costs the overage premium: Rs 3.52 - Rs 1.60
      = Rs 1.92 per GB. Declaring too much costs the wasted commitment:
      Rs 1.60 per GB. The two are not equal, so the best guess is NOT
      the average.

  M3  LET THE PRICES PICK THE QUANTILE
      Cost of being short over cost of being short plus cost of being
      long:

          1.92 / (1.92 + 1.60) = 0.545

      Declare the 55th percentile of next month's usage.
      Not the mean. Not the mean plus a safety margin.

  M4  FORECAST WHAT IS BILLED
      The invoice is in gigabytes, not listening hours. Forecast
      gigabytes. Any proxy you convert through a ratio will drift, and
      you will be wrong in a direction you cannot see.

  M5  SCORE WHAT YOU WOULD OTHERWISE SHIP
      Replay history: what would the spreadsheet have cost? What would
      "declare last month's number" have cost? Price both in rupees.
      That is the bar your model has to clear, and if it does not clear
      it, you ship the simpler thing.


THE ANSWER, IN ONE PASS
-----------------------
The decision is a single monthly number, locked 30 days out, with an
asymmetric penalty. So the output cannot be a point forecast - it has to
be a distribution, and I read one quantile off it.

The quantile comes from the contract, not from taste. Being one GB short
costs Rs 1.92 extra; one wasted GB costs Rs 1.60. That fraction is 0.545,
so I declare the P55 of next month's gigabytes.

That result is worth sitting with, because it contradicts the instinct.
Everyone's reflex with a penalty attached is to declare high. The
arithmetic says declare barely above the median, because 2.2x is a mild
penalty. The 15% safety margin is not caution, it is systematic
overpayment - which is exactly why 8 months had unused commitment. And
going over in 4 of 12 months is not a failure: at the optimum we EXPECT
to go over about 45% of the time. Somebody needs to hear that plainly.

I forecast daily gigabytes and sum to the month, rather than forecasting
monthly totals directly. Three years of history is about 1,100 daily
observations but only 36 monthly ones, and the daily series carries the
weekly cycle that drives the sum.

For the model I start with seasonal naive - this week looks like last
week - and take the distribution from its own historical errors. Then I
replay the last twelve months: what would the spreadsheet have cost, what
would seasonal-naive-at-P55 have cost. I expect most of the saving to
come from the quantile, not from the model, because today's failure is
the absence of a distribution, not the absence of machine learning.

Only if that is not enough do I climb to something with parameters in it.


WHAT TRANSFERS
--------------
  M1  Find the decision
      -> Every ML problem. "What action changes?" kills more bad
         projects than any other question.

  M2  Price both directions
      + -> Fraud: what does a blocked good customer cost vs a missed
  M3  Pick the quantile        fraudster?
      -> Moderation: what does a wrongly removed post cost vs a missed
         harmful one? That ratio sets the auto-remove threshold.
      -> Inventory, staffing, capacity: the same fractile, every time.
      -> Any time you are about to choose a threshold by eye, or report
         a mean, ask what the two errors cost. This is the single most
         portable idea in the repo.

  M4  Forecast what is billed
      -> Label design everywhere. Predict the quantity the decision is
         denominated in. Recommenders trained on clicks when the
         business is paid in purchases are the same mistake.

  M5  Score what you would otherwise ship
      -> The incumbent is a model, even when it is a human with a
         spreadsheet. Score it. Report your lift against it in money.
